CHAPTER I

Allah — Before All Creation

الله — قبل كل شيء

Before time existed, before space had dimension, before a single atom had been called into being — there was only Allah. He has no beginning and no end. He was not contained by anything, for nothing else existed. He did not reside in a place, for no place had been made. He simply was — and always has been — Al-Awwal (The First) and Al-Akhir (The Last), Al-Zahir (The Manifest) and Al-Batin (The Hidden).

"He is the First and the Last, the Evident and the Hidden: and He has full knowledge of all things."

Surah Al-Hadid (57:3)

The companions of the Prophet ﷺ once asked: where was Allah before He created the heavens and the earth? The Prophet ﷺ answered with extraordinary precision — not with philosophy, not with theology, but with a single word that the scholars of Islam have spent centuries contemplating.

"He was in 'amaa — and there was no air above Him and no air below Him. Then He created His Throne upon the water."

Sunan al-Tirmidhi (hasan) · Sunan Ibn Majah · Narrated by Abu Razin al-Uqayli RA

'Amaa — a word that defies simple translation. Scholars describe it as a sublime, imperceptible state of existence beyond any human frame of reference. A state that is not darkness, not void, not nothingness — but something that belongs entirely to Allah, before categories and conditions were invented.

He had no need to create. His decision to bring the universe into existence was an act of pure, absolute, uncaused will. Nothing compelled Him. Nothing preceded His decision. Nothing advised Him.

"He is not asked about what He does, but they will be asked."

Surah Al-Anbiya (21:23)

CHAPTER II

The Pen & The Preserved Tablet

القلم واللوح المحفوظ

The first thing Allah created was not the sun. Not the earth. Not the angels. Not the heavens. The first act of creation was the act of writing.

"The first thing Allah created was the Pen. He said to it: Write. It asked: My Lord, what shall I write? Allah said: Write the decree of everything that will occur until the Hour comes."

Sunan al-Tirmidhi (hasan sahih) · Authenticated by al-Albani · Also in Sunan Abu Dawud

The Pen wrote upon Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz — the Preserved Tablet. Everything that has ever happened, is happening now, and will happen until the Day of Judgement was inscribed in that single primordial act of writing — 50,000 years before the heavens and earth were created.

"Allah had written the decrees of all creation fifty thousand years before He created the heavens and the earth, while His Throne was upon the water."

Sahih Muslim (2653) · Narrated by Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As RA

"Nun. By the Pen and what they inscribe."

Surah Al-Qalam (68:1) — The Quran opens Surah al-Qalam by swearing an oath upon the Pen itself

The profound significance: the first thing Allah brought into existence was not matter, not energy, not space — but knowledge recorded. Decree. Order. Before a single particle existed, the universe was already written. Every death, every birth, every grain of sand, every heartbeat — already documented in the Preserved Tablet before time began.

SCHOLARLY NOTE

Ibn Kathir writes in Al-Bidayah wa'l-Nihayah (Vol. 1): "The Pen is the first of the created things, and it inscribed upon the Preserved Tablet all that was to be until the Day of Resurrection. This is the basis of the Islamic theology of qadar — divine decree — which is one of the six pillars of faith."

CHAPTER III

The Throne, Water & The Seven Heavens

العرش والكرسي والسموات السبع

After the Pen had written all that was decreed, Allah created Al-Arsh — The Throne. The most magnificent of all physical creations. A structure so vast that the seven heavens and the earth, compared to the Throne, are like a ring tossed into a desert.

"And it is He who created the heavens and the earth in six days — and His Throne was upon the water — that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed."

Surah Hud (11:7)

The Throne rested upon water. Before the earth was formed, before the atmosphere existed, before any planet had condensed — there was Allah's Throne upon a primordial water. The scholars of Islam note this is the same water mentioned in the Pen hadith: the Throne was created, and then placed upon the water, and the Pen had already written all things.

"His Kursiy (Footstool) extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great."

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:255) — Ayat al-Kursi, the greatest verse in the Quran

The distinction between the Arsh and the Kursiy is profound. Ibn Abbas RA said: "The Kursiy is the place of the two Feet, and no one can estimate the greatness of the Throne." The Kursiy alone encompasses all the heavens and the earth. The Arsh is greater still.

THE SEVEN HEAVENS

"Then He directed Himself toward the heaven while it was smoke, and said to it and to the earth: Come willingly or by compulsion. They said: We have come willingly. And He completed them as seven heavens within two days."

Surah Fussilat (41:11-12)

The heavens were once a singular smoke — a unified mass — before Allah separated and ordered them into seven layered heavens. Modern cosmologists describe the early universe as a plasma and then a gaseous state before structure formed. The Quran described this, in the 7th century, as "smoke." Each heaven is distinct, inhabited, administered by angels, separated by distances so vast they defeat human imagination.

CHAPTER IV

The Angels — Created from Light

الملائكة — خُلقوا من النور

"The angels were created from light, the jinn were created from smokeless fire, and Adam was created from what has been described to you."

Sahih Muslim (2996) · Narrated by Aisha RA

Before humanity, before the earth, before the dinosaurs, before the first single-celled organism — the angels existed. Created from pure light, they are beings of absolute obedience, incapable of disobeying Allah, constantly engaged in His worship and the execution of His commands.

"Praise be to Allah, Creator of the heavens and the earth, who made the angels messengers with wings — two or three or four. He increases in creation what He wills. Indeed, Allah is over all things competent."

Surah Fatir (35:1)
Jibril AS

The greatest of angels. Charged with delivering divine revelation to all prophets. Described as having 600 wings, each wing filling the horizon. The Prophet ﷺ saw him twice in his true form. (Sahih Bukhari 4856)

Israfil AS

Holds the Trumpet (Al-Sur). Awaits the command to blow — the first blast will end all of creation, the second will raise it. He has held the trumpet to his lips, bowed, his eyes fixed on the Throne, since he was created.

Mikail AS

Responsible for rain and all provisions. Under his command: every drop of water that falls, every seed that sprouts, every river that flows to the ocean.

Izra'il AS

The Angel of Death. Takes every soul at its appointed moment — from the greatest king to the smallest insect. His task has no pause and no end until the Trumpet sounds.

Malik

Guardian of Jahannam (Hell). "They will call: O Malik, let your Lord put an end to us! He will say: Indeed, you will remain." (Quran 43:77)

Ridwan

Guardian of Jannah (Paradise). Custodian of the gardens that no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human mind has conceived.

Hamalat al-Arsh

The eight angels who bear Allah's Throne on the Day of Judgement. The distance between each one's earlobe and shoulder equals a 700-year journey. (Ibn Kathir, Al-Bidayah wa'l-Nihayah, Vol. 1)

CHAPTER V

The Jinn — Created Before Humanity

الجن — خُلقوا قبل بني آدم

"And the jinn We created before [humans] from scorching fire."

Surah Al-Hijr (15:27)

"And He created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire."

Surah Al-Rahman (55:15)

The jinn inhabited this earth long before the first human set foot upon it. Like humans, they possess free will, reason, accountability, and a Day of Judgement. They eat, procreate, and die. They live among us — unseen — in a parallel dimension of existence. Surah Al-Jinn (72) is devoted entirely to a group of jinn who heard the Quran recited and accepted Islam.

Abdullah ibn Amr RA narrated: "The jinn were on Earth two thousand years before the creation of Adam, and they caused mischief therein, shedding blood and killing one another."

Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Quran al-Azim on Surah 2:30 · Al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan on Surah 2:30

This narration provides the answer to one of the most debated questions in Islamic theology: why did the angels say what they said when Allah announced the creation of Adam AS? The angels had witnessed the jinn — the previous inhabitants of Earth — spread corruption and shed blood. They were not objecting. They were asking, in reverence, with the evidence before their eyes: will this new creation be different?

"And [mention] when your Lord said to the angels: Indeed, I will make upon the earth a successive authority. They said: Will You place upon it one who causes corruption therein and sheds blood? Allah said: Indeed, I know that which you do not know."

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:30)

CHAPTER VI

Iblis — Pride Before the Fall

إبليس — أول المعصية

Among the jinn was one who had elevated himself, through millennia of worship, to a station among the angels. He worshipped with them, stood alongside them, and had been given a position of great honour. His name was Iblis. He was not an angel — he was a jinn. But he had earned proximity to the divine court through sheer dedication of worship.

"And [mention] when We said to the angels: Prostrate to Adam. And they prostrated, except for Iblis. He was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord."

Surah Al-Kahf (18:50)

When Allah commanded all to prostrate before Adam AS — the angels obeyed without question. Iblis refused. In that refusal was contained the seed of every evil that has ever come to pass in human history.

"He said: I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay."

Surah Al-A'raf (7:12) · Surah Sad (38:76)

The scholars of Islam identify this moment as the origin of kibr — arrogance. Not disbelief first. Not disobedience first. Pride first. The first sin ever committed in creation was not a physical act. It was a thought: "I am better than him." Iblis compared himself to Adam AS, found himself superior by material composition — fire over clay — and let that comparison override the command of Allah. He chose his own judgment over divine decree.

"[Allah] said: Descend from it — for it is not for you to be arrogant therein. So get out; indeed, you are of the debased."

Surah Al-A'raf (7:13)

Expelled from the heavens, Iblis requested a reprieve — to remain alive until the Day of Judgement. Allah granted it. And Iblis made a declaration that would define all of human history to follow:

"He said: Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path. Then I will come to them from before them and from behind them and on their right and on their left, and You will not find most of them grateful."

Surah Al-A'raf (7:16-17)
THEOLOGICAL POINT

Islam does not hold that Iblis is an equal or opposite force to good. He is not a god of evil. He is a creation of Allah — granted free will, who chose arrogance, was given respite, and now tests humanity as part of Allah's decree. The test itself is the wisdom: would humans choose what Iblis rejected? Would they prostrate when commanded?

CHAPTER VII

The Earth — Six Stages of Creation

خلق الأرض — ستة أيام

The Prophet ﷺ narrated, in one of the most precise cosmological accounts in all of Islamic literature, the sequence in which this earth was prepared for its ultimate inhabitant — Adam AS:

"Allah created the earth, then created the mountains, then created the trees, then created what is disliked, then created light, then scattered the animals throughout it, then — in the last hour of the last day — created Adam AS, after 'Asr, in the last moment before nightfall."

Sahih Muslim (2789) · Narrated by Abu Hurayrah RA
1
The Earth Itself

The foundational globe — land, rock, and the primordial waters. The stage upon which all subsequent creation would be placed.

2
The Mountains

"And He has cast into the earth firmly set mountains, lest it shift with you." (Quran 16:15). Stabilizers of the earth, driven deep into its crust like pegs.

3
The Trees & Plant Life

All vegetation, from the tallest tree to the smallest grass. The oxygen-producing, soil-binding, food-providing foundation of biological life.

4
What is Disliked — Trials & Difficulty

Hardship, pain, adversity — the necessary counterweight that makes gratitude meaningful and patience an act of worship.

5
Light

"Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth." (Quran 24:35). The illumination of existence itself — physical light and the light of guidance.

6
Animals — Then Adam AS

All living creatures were scattered across the earth. And then, in the very last moment of the sixth and final stage — after 'Asr, before the setting of the sun — Adam AS was created. The last of all creation. The crown.

ON THE "SIX DAYS"

The Quran mentions that the heavens and earth were created in six "days" (Quran 7:54, 10:3, 25:59, 32:4, 50:38, 57:4). The scholars note that a "day" with Allah is as 50,000 years of our reckoning (Quran 70:4), or as 1,000 years (Quran 32:5). These are not Earth's 24-hour days. The universe is vast and ancient — by Allah's own design and wisdom.

CHAPTER VIII

What Other Traditions Say

ما تقوله التقاليد الأخرى

Across every major religious tradition, humanity has grappled with the same question: what was there before? Below is how the other great Abrahamic traditions and wider schools of thought answer it — with precision and without caricature.

JUDAISM — THE TORAH

Genesis/Bereshit 1–2 describes creation in six literal days. God creates by speech: "Vayomer Elohim" (And God said). Light and darkness on Day 1, the sky on Day 2, dry land and plants on Day 3, the sun and stars on Day 4, fish and birds on Day 5, land animals and Adam on Day 6. The traditional Hebrew calendar date of creation: 3761 BCE (Year 1, Anno Mundi). Maimonides (12th century CE), the greatest Jewish philosopher, held that Genesis need not be interpreted literally — the "days" may be allegorical for vast ages.

CHRISTIANITY — GENESIS

Christianity inherits the Genesis narrative with four major interpretive traditions:

Young Earth Creationism: Literal six days; universe ~6,000 years old.
Old Earth Creationism: "Days" represent geological ages — billions of years permitted.
Day-Age Theory: Each "day" equals an era.
Theistic Evolution: God created the universe and life through the process of evolution. The dominant position of the Catholic Church (Pope John Paul II, 1996) and most mainline Protestant denominations. The Catholic Catechism §283 states creation questions "invite us to even greater admiration for the greatness of the Creator."

HINDUISM

In Hindu cosmology, the universe is cyclical — without a single beginning or end. Brahma creates the universe, Vishnu sustains it, Shiva destroys it — and the cycle repeats. One full cycle (kalpa) equals 4.32 billion years, remarkably close to the scientific age of the Earth (4.5 billion years). The universe is currently in the Kali Yuga — the final, darkest age before the next creation cycle begins.

ZOROASTRIANISM

Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord) created the world in six stages — remarkably parallel to the Abrahamic six-day account. The cosmic struggle between Ahura Mazda (good) and Angra Mainyu (evil) shapes history. Concepts of heaven, hell, angels, demons, a final judgment, and bodily resurrection all appear in Zoroastrianism centuries before the Christian era — influencing Judaism during the Babylonian exile and subsequently Christianity and Islam, according to comparative religion scholars.

CHAPTER IX

Science — The Universe Unfolds

كيف تصف العلوم الكون
A NOTE FOR MUSLIM READERS

The scientific account does not conflict with Islamic theology at the fundamental level. The Quran does not prescribe a specific age for the Earth or universe. Its "days" of creation are acknowledged by scholars — Ibn Abbas RA, Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir — as not meaning 24-hour days. The Quran repeatedly commands observation of the natural world: "Do they not look at the sky above them, how We constructed it?" (50:6). "Travel through the earth and observe." (29:20). Islamic civilisation produced the world's greatest scientists because the pursuit of knowledge was considered an act of worship.

THE BIG BANG — 13.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

The universe began from a singularity — all matter, energy, space and time erupting outward in a single moment. Before the Big Bang: no space, no time, no matter. Not "nothing" in the human sense — a state that defies the categories our minds use.

Sources: NASA Astrophysics · Planck Collaboration 2015 · Hawking, A Brief History of Time

"Do the disbelievers not see that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, then We separated them?"

Surah Al-Anbiya (21:30) — Noted by astrophysicist Dr. Maurice Bucaille and others as a precise description of the Big Bang, written in the 7th century CE
THE EARTH — 4.5 BILLION YEARS AGO

A nebula of gas and dust collapses under gravity. The Sun ignites. Rocky material accretes into planets. Earth forms from the collision and fusion of smaller bodies — molten, bombarded by asteroids. The Moon forms from a catastrophic collision with a Mars-sized object (Theia). Over millions of years, Earth cools, oceans form from water vapour condensing and asteroid delivery.

Sources: USGS Geological Survey · Canup, R.M., Formation of the Moon (Science, 2012)
FIRST LIFE — 3.8 BILLION YEARS AGO

Single-celled organisms — prokaryotes — appear in Earth's oceans. Photosynthetic bacteria begin producing oxygen. The Great Oxidation Event (~2.4 billion years ago) permanently transforms Earth's atmosphere, making complex life possible.

Sources: Schopf, J.W., Microfossils of early Archean Apex Chert (Nature, 1993)
THE AGE OF DINOSAURS — 252 to 66 MILLION YEARS AGO

The Permian-Triassic extinction (~252 million years ago) wipes out 96% of all marine species. Life recovers. Dinosaurs emerge (~230 million years ago), diversify into every ecological niche, and dominate the earth for 165 million years. Birds evolve from theropod dinosaurs during the Jurassic period. Then: an asteroid 10 kilometres wide strikes the Yucatan Peninsula (Mexico) — the Chicxulub impactor. Equivalent energy: 10 billion atomic bombs. Firestorms. An impact winter lasting years. 75% of all species go extinct. The dinosaurs are gone. Birds survive. Small mammals survive. The stage is cleared for what comes next.

Sources: Alvarez et al., Chicxulub Crater (Science, 1980) · Smithsonian Human Origins · Nature Geoscience
HUMAN EVOLUTION — 300,000 YEARS AGO TO ADAM AS

Homo sapiens — anatomically modern humans — appear in Africa approximately 300,000 years ago (Jebel Irhoud fossils, Morocco). Behavioural modernity — art, language, complex culture — emerges ~70,000 years ago. Humans spread across every continent. And then, at a specific moment in this story — which only Allah knows — He created Adam AS directly, breathed His Ruh (spirit) into him, taught him the names of all things, and placed him in Jannah before sending him to lead humanity on Earth. The biological account and the theological account need not contradict: where science ends, revelation begins.

Sources: Hublin et al., New fossils from Jebel Irhoud (Nature, 2017) · Human Genome Project · Smithsonian

CHAPTER X

The Announcement of Adam AS

إعلان خلق آدم عليه السلام

Everything that preceded — the Pen, the Preserved Tablet, the Throne, the seven heavens, the earth's formation across its six stages, the angels, the jinn, the expulsion of Iblis — all of it was, in a sense, preparation. Not by necessity. Allah needed none of it. But by design, by wisdom, by a plan inscribed in the Preserved Tablet before time began, everything was being made ready.

"And [mention] when your Lord said to the angels: Indeed, I will make upon the earth a khalifah (vicegerent, steward)."

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:30)

The angels, having witnessed the jinn's chaos on Earth, asked a question not of objection but of wonder. Allah's answer — "I know what you do not know" — is among the most profound statements in the Quran. It does not explain. It does not justify. It simply reminds: there is knowledge before which all created minds are children.

Then Allah created Adam AS from the dust of the earth — gathering clay from every part of it. Which is why human beings differ: in colour, in temperament, in nature. Some are dark as the deep soil of Africa, some pale as the chalk of Europe, some golden as the sands of Arabia. All from the same clay. All equal before Allah except in taqwa.

"Allah created Adam from a handful of dust that He took from all over the earth. So the children of Adam came forth according to the earth: some came out red, some white, some black, some between, some soft, some coarse, some evil, some good."

Sunan Abu Dawud (4693) · Sunan al-Tirmidhi · Authenticated hadith · Narrated by Abu Musa al-Ash'ari RA

Allah breathed His Ruh — His spirit — into Adam AS. The clay became a living being. He stood upright. He sneezed. He said: Al-hamdulillah — Praise be to Allah. And Allah replied: Yarhamuk Allah — May Allah have mercy on you.

The first words ever spoken by a human being were the praise of Allah. The first response ever given to a human being was the mercy of Allah. In those two exchanges, the entire human story was encoded: we are creatures who praise; we are recipients of mercy.

"And He taught Adam the names of all things, then showed them to the angels and said: Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful. They said: Exalted are You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us. Indeed, it is You who is the Knowing, the Wise."

Surah Al-Baqarah (2:31-32)

Allah then asked Adam AS to name everything before the angels. He knew every name — every creature, every object, every concept. The angels, limitless in worship but bounded in knowledge, could only say: We know only what You have taught us.

This was the demonstration. The reason humanity was chosen for the earth was not strength. Not beauty. Not material superiority. It was knowledge — the capacity to learn, name, understand, and build upon what was learned. This capacity — unique among creation — is the gift that carries the burden of accountability.

"We have certainly created the human being in the best of forms."

Surah Al-Tin (95:4)

The story of everything before Adam AS is the story of preparation. The story of Adam AS is the story of a beginning. And every human being who has ever lived is a continuation of what began on that afternoon — after 'Asr, in the last light of the sixth day — when Allah breathed life into clay, and history began.
